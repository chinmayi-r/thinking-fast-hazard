Thinking Fast Enough on HAZARD

Update for Tom, Nov 17 · CR · every number labelled by machine and snapshot in NOTES.md

The question

A robot has a reflex that answers in no time and a slow brain that may answer better. Thinking is charged on the same clock as the world (30 frames per second of wall time, RRARA's rule). Does a gate between the two beat either alone?

  • Nothing beats the reflex. RRARA's checker costs a few points; its overrides are never wiser than the rule. A fast model can replace the hand reflex, which is the one positive.

Why the current snapshot scores 0.1 for everyone: three body defects

Each found in the code, verified in saved map arrays, fixed behind a flag with unit tests.

  • The walk loop creeps. Each step sends the body to the path's first corner, usually 13 cm away, with a 50 cm arrival radius: it turns, walks 5 cm, reports success, re-plans. "Max steps" is a 100-frame budget, so a walk nets about 1 m. The paper's snapshot has the same loop; its 1.25 m arrival forgives one more metre, which is most of the gap between 0.7 there and 0.1 here. Fix: a lookahead waypoint, 2 m per 100 frames measured.
  • The ceiling is recorded as walls. Fix: ignore points above body height. Rule agent, 25 kitchens, clock on: 0.230 with the fix vs 0.131 shipped, paired +0.10.
  • Phantom objects on the map border. Out-of-map points are clamped to the border, so far-plane pixels around an object's silhouette paint copies of it 25 m away; HAZARD's prompt reports the centre of those cells as "distance", so a comb 2 m away reads 30 m. Every brain that reads distances is fooled; random reads nothing, which is why random holds up here. Fix: drop out-of-map points.

Also found: HAZARD's heading function returns the mirror of the true heading; our honest text had left and right swapped until this morning (fixed, tests).

Honest text and the human ceiling

HAZARD's prompt gives distances in cells, hides why a walk failed and prints a literal placeholder for history. Rewritten to say what a navigation stack knows: metres, bearings, feasibility, stop reason, full history, the cost of each option. With it Jev saves items on 3a-1 where before it only looked around. Played by hand with unlimited time, CR and I both score 0.222 on 3a-1; with the three fixes I reach three items but two have already burned. The worth-5 item there sits on a fire source and is gone before frame 50. The ceiling is the fire, not the brain.

Liane's loop

Built end to end. Round 1 compiled by Opus from Haiku's traces: 0.204 on five training scenes; round 2, with round 1's program and validation in the prompt: 0.294. Both run on the 20 held-out kitchens this afternoon.

What it shows

On HAZARD fire the best policy is "always run the reflex", and that is the task, not the implementation: nearest valuable item first is the right answer, so deliberation has no decision to improve and a gate has nothing to protect. A clean negative with the reason named, and the specification of a testbed that can show the gate: decisions where the greedy choice is wrong often enough that a slow solver, given time, scores well above a reflex, and where that time costs something. Everything built this month transfers unchanged.

Decisions for today

  1. Keep HAZARD as the latency testbed; report the reflex ceiling and the three body defects.
  2. Design HAZARD scenes where greedy is wrong (a far high-value item that burns first, items behind doors), and run flood and wind under the clock, where the paper shows the reflex losing.
  3. Move to a benchmark that needs lookahead, as Karthik suggested, and port the clock and the gate to it.
